Auto-Apply Voucher for Shopee Live checkout

Applying the host's voucher automatically took 42 seconds. Entering it by hand took 1 minute 6 seconds.

Type
Bootcamp case on a real product. The baseline numbers come from the case data, which the deck labels as a dummy case study. Nothing was shipped.
Role
Individual work across three mini projects: strategy, research and requirements, experiment and PRD
When
August to September 2026
Keywords
E-commerce, live commerce, product research, BRD and FSD, usability testing, PRD, RICE

The problem

In the case data, Shopee Live converts 1.8% of viewers. 70% drop off on the voucher and payment page, and a checkout takes 7 to 9 clicks.

I looked at 45 live transactions. Failed checkouts took 101.7 seconds and successful ones 54.5 seconds, so failures were 1.9 times slower. Checkout time rose with the number of buyers at once and fell as more stock remained. Both people I interviewed named typing a voucher code by hand as one of the slow steps.

My reading: during a flash sale, slow buyers lose to faster ones. Stock runs out while they are still entering a code.

What I did

  1. Strategy deck. Problems, solutions, customer segments, market sizing and OKR. All labelled educational, with dummy case data.
  2. BRD and FSD (14 August). Scope, requirements, constraints and stakeholders for the feature.
  3. Experiment plan and test (27 to 28 August). A moderated usability test on a prototype I built in Lovable.
  4. PRD (last updated 5 September). Evidence, hypothesis, competitor benchmark, RICE-scored user stories, user flow, out of scope and open questions.

Decisions

Why vouchers first. I had three directions: auto-apply vouchers, holding stock during checkout, and locking the live-sale price. I picked vouchers as the quick win because it only changes the checkout screen and the promo rules. It does not touch inventory. The other two stayed as separate initiatives.

Against keeping manual entry. Some platforms keep manual codes on purpose, because claiming a discount feels like winning. I judged that matters less in a live sale, where traffic is high and stock is limited.

Competitor check. Lazada's collectible vouchers already apply automatically. TikTok Shop applies the best available discount, but its live-session vouchers must still be claimed first. That gap is where auto-apply fits.

The test

Two prototypes, one participant each. Both were students aged 17 to 25 who use Shopee Live. Variant A has an empty voucher box and an Apply button. Variant B has no box and shows a badge: "Voucher Host Diterapkan Otomatis".

A: by handB: automatic
Time to finish voucher and payment1 min 6 s42 s
Clicks54

Variant A's click count does not include typing the code.

The participant on A hunted for the voucher box and had to remember the code. The participant on B finished faster but took a few seconds to notice the voucher was already applied. When I asked whether it was the best voucher for the order, they said they were not sure.

What changed after the test

The doubt about whether it was the best voucher was the trade-off I had listed before the test, and it showed up in real behaviour. So the PRD gained a second feature: a badge that says the best voucher has been applied, with the discount amount. It scores RICE 3,900 against 4,550 for auto-apply itself.

Two things stayed out of scope for now: a way to view voucher details, and a notification when the host changes the voucher mid-session. The BRD from 14 August had a manual override in scope. By the PRD I had left detail viewing for a later iteration and listed the badge question as open.

Still open: which voucher wins when several are active, what happens if the voucher runs out at payment, and whether a badge alone is enough to restore trust.

What this does not show. One person per variant is a direction, not an effect size. The PRD says so: the effect on conversion cannot be estimated from two participants.
