Which format should AromaBrew open in Yogyakarta?

A grab-and-go kiosk pays back in about 2.9 months. The café format takes 10.

Type
Case exercise. The figures come from case exhibits provided by 180 Degrees Consulting UGM. Not a real engagement.
Role
Individual recommendation deck
Keywords
Food and beverage, market entry, payback analysis, risk and mitigation, recommendation deck

The question

AromaBrew, a Jakarta coffee brand, is considering a branch in Yogyakarta. The client asked three things: is Yogyakarta still viable, which entry strategy fits, and how to avoid financial losses.

In the clarifying answers, the client's main goal for year one was full payback as fast as possible. It cannot afford a long stretch without profit. The market is crowded: over 3,500 coffee shops, and local aesthetic cafés charge about 30% less for a similar experience.

The comparison

Per monthA: Co-working caféB: Grab-and-go kiosk
Initial investmentRp 300MRp 100M
RevenueRp 157.5MRp 108M
Total costRp 127.5MRp 73M
ProfitRp 30MRp 35M
Payback10.0 monthsabout 2.9 months

Revenue is price per cup × daily customers × 30 days. A: Rp 35,000 × 150. B: Rp 18,000 × 200. Payback is investment divided by monthly profit.

Option A earns more revenue but carries Rp 60M of fixed cost a month against Rp 25M for B. B makes more profit on a third of the capital.

Recommendation

Enter through the kiosk, under a distinct sub-brand.

  • It meets the client's stated priority: fast payback.
  • It risks Rp 100M instead of Rp 300M in a price-sensitive market.
  • Tourists in Yogyakarta want quick, affordable coffee, which suits a kiosk better than a sit-down concept.

The trade-off. To reach the cost target, the kiosk uses a commercial house blend instead of AromaBrew's single-origin beans, the thing that built its Jakarta name. A separate sub-brand, such as an "Express" line, keeps quality expectations apart from the flagship.

What could go wrong

RiskMitigation
Brand dilution from cheaper beansDistinct sub-brand
Price war with local cafésCompete on speed and convenience for tourists, not on price alone
200 customers a day is unproven for a new entrantReview after the first 2 to 3 months before scaling

Limits of the numbers. I used the exhibit data as given. Daily customers are assumed at full level from month 1, with no ramp-up, and payback counts monthly profit only. So 2.9 months is what the numbers give if footfall holds from day one.
