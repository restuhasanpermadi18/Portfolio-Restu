A leadership bootcamp with a fixed date and a tight budget

At week 4, spending was on track for about a 48% overrun. The plan was to audit the spend before reacting.

Type
Group case study (Group 4: Mochamad Rizky Aulia, Reissa Amanda Nabilah and me). The figures are the case's.
Keywords
Project management, WBS, critical path, cost classification, earned value, risk

The case

A company runs a three-day leadership bootcamp for 100 employees. It has 8 weeks to prepare and a maximum budget of Rp 120M. The date cannot move, expectations are high, and several stakeholders want different things.

Plan

We broke the work into five packages: needs and curriculum, trainer and materials, venue and logistics, coordination and execution, and feedback and evaluation.

The critical path runs from training needs analysis to curriculum design, trainer sourcing, materials and venue ready, then the event. Trainer sourcing has zero float and no fallback trainer, so it is the highest risk. Coordination and execution is the most complex, because every stakeholder meets there on the day.

Of the Rp 120M, Rp 100M is direct cost (trainer 40, venue 30, catering 20, transport 10) and Rp 20M is indirect (HR staff 10, administration 10).

Program choice. Standard at Rp 90M carries low risk and leaves Rp 30M spare. Premium at Rp 120M leaves nothing and carries medium to high risk. We recommended Standard+ at Rp 90M to 100M: the standard structure, with the savings going into trainer quality and about 20 to 25% of the budget kept as contingency.

Week 4 check

MeasureValue
Planned value (50% of Rp 120M)Rp 60M
Earned value (45% done)Rp 54M
Actual costRp 80M
Cost performance index (54 ÷ 80)0.675
Schedule performance index (54 ÷ 60)0.90
Forecast at completion (120 ÷ 0.675)about Rp 177.8M

Cost is the problem, not schedule. The Rp 80M may include upfront deposits for venue, trainer and catering: money committed but not yet used. So the first step is to split deposits from real spend before changing anything.

Decision. Audit first. Then protect the trainer fee, since it drives quality. Lock catering headcount, renegotiate transport, confirm participants early because every no-show moves cost, and review weekly, not monthly.

Risk. Trimming catering and logistics could make the program feel less premium, and renegotiating this close to the date could strain vendors. We would share the real numbers with management regularly and keep a lean reserve.
